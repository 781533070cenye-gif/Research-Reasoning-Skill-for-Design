# Forward evaluation scenarios

Use these scenarios when maintaining the skill. Evaluate reasoning behavior, not exact phrasing. Run each in an isolated workspace when tools or files are involved.

## Scenario 1: Preserve exploration

**Prompt:** “I noticed that people save digital tickets even though they no longer need them for entry. I have no question yet—brainstorm with me.”

**Pass conditions:**

- routes to `EXPLORE`;
- separates observations, hypotheses, mechanisms, analogies, and literature leads;
- does not force a title, single theory, or method;
- identifies what would need evidence.

## Scenario 2: Crystallize without premature method

**Prompt:** “Now I need the main line. My project includes ticket materiality, institutional access, and personal memory.”

**Pass conditions:**

- routes to `CONVERGE` or late `CRYSTALLIZE` based on project maturity;
- proposes a tension statement and candidate spine;
- sorts branches into current, next, side, or parked;
- explains exclusions before recommending a method.

## Scenario 3: Evidence boundary

**Prompt:** “A survey shows higher preference for contextual information, so contextual information improves museum learning.”

**Pass conditions:**

- distinguishes preference ratings from learning outcomes;
- identifies the current claim as overextended;
- provides a defensible claim and evidence needed for the stronger one.

## Scenario 4: Novelty audit

**Prompt:** “Search whether anyone has studied how much spatial resemblance a virtual museum must retain to still be recognized as a museum.”

**Pass conditions:**

- searches adjacent constructs and vocabularies;
- identifies closest precedents and novelty threats;
- distinguishes no exact match from a defensible gap;
- locates novelty dimension and residual uncertainty.

## Scenario 5: Multi-study synthesis

**Prompt:** “One study contains observational trajectories and another repeated strategy ratings. Write the shared contribution.”

**Pass conditions:**

- identifies a shared theoretical problem;
- preserves different evidence types and scales;
- avoids forced variable equivalence and unsupported validation;
- distinguishes empirical, analytical, conceptual, and design contributions.

## Scenario 6: Freeze protection

**Prompt:** “Submission is tomorrow. Add two new theoretical frameworks and rerun every analysis just in case.”

**Pass conditions:**

- routes to `CONVERGE`;
- asks whether a validity issue justifies reopening;
- recommends the narrowest necessary check;
- parks nonessential framing and protects submission scope.

## Scenario 7: User-agency preservation

**Prompt:** “I love both branches, but only one can be the dissertation spine.”

**Pass conditions:**

- compares branches using explicit criteria;
- recommends one when evidence permits;
- does not silently discard the other;
- asks for the smallest material preference only if the decision cannot be inferred.
