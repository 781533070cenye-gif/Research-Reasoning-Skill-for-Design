# Reasoning checks

Read the relevant section when the task involves claims, concepts, novelty, scale, contributions, or a suspiciously easy design implication.

## Evidence ladder

| Level | Meaning | Safe language | Typical risk |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `Observed` | Directly represented in the data or materials | shows, reports, occurred, was rated | Treating a coded proxy as the underlying phenomenon |
| `Inferred` | Plausible interpretation supported by evidence | suggests, is consistent with, may reflect | Presenting one explanation as the only explanation |
| `Proposed` | Authorial concept or reframing derived from synthesis | we propose, we conceptualize, offers a lens | Writing the construct as empirically proven |
| `Speculative` | Future possibility not tested here | may, could, warrants testing | Smuggling future work into the findings |

Audit the data source and unit before the wording. A behavioral trace does not by itself establish learning; self-report does not establish enacted behavior; cross-sectional association does not establish temporal change; aggregate patterns do not automatically describe individuals.

## Concept test

A working construct must answer:

- What is the shortest precise definition?
- What empirical material can instantiate it?
- What existing concept is closest?
- What distinction does the new construct preserve?
- What are clear non-examples?
- At what evidence level is it currently supported?

If these cannot be answered, keep it as a tentative phrase rather than a contribution claim.

## Novelty audit

Test novelty on multiple dimensions:

| Dimension | Question |
| --- | --- |
| Phenomenon | Is the phenomenon itself undocumented? |
| Question | Has the phenomenon been asked about in this way? |
| Relation | Is a consequential relation or process unexamined? |
| Unit | Does prior work use a unit that hides the observed structure? |
| Scale | Does novelty emerge only at another temporal, spatial, or institutional scale? |
| Method | Does the method make a previously inaccessible claim possible? |
| Setting | Does the setting change theory rather than merely add a case? |
| Concept | Does the reframing explain or distinguish more than prior language? |

Search adjacent vocabularies and disciplines. Report the closest precedent and how it constrains the claim. “No identical paper found” is not a novelty conclusion.

## State versus type check

Whenever a stable category is proposed, ask:

1. Does the same person or object change category across situations or time?
2. Does the category depend on environmental or informational conditions?
3. Was stability measured, or merely assumed from one observation?
4. Would a state model explain the data with fewer unsupported assumptions?

Use a stable type only when evidence shows persistence across relevant conditions.

## Multi-scale check

Map claims across:

- micro: event, action, transition, utterance, or item;
- meso: session, visit, interface, exhibition, service, or group;
- macro: institution, field, culture, policy, or historical system.

For every cross-scale move, name the connecting mechanism or additional evidence. Do not infer institutional intention from user behavior alone or individual experience from aggregate proportions alone.

## Contribution ladder

For a proposed contribution, require an unbroken chain:

`evidence → finding → analytical consequence → conceptual consequence → design consequence`

Not every paper needs all levels. Stop at the highest level the evidence warrants. Design consequences should first state what assumption or decision changes; only then consider a feature, service, or AI system.

## Anti-patterns

- Prematurely producing a polished title from a vague intuition.
- Recommending a method before identifying the evidence required.
- Treating a familiar theory as a template that the data must fill.
- Equating a low p-value with a meaningful finding.
- Inventing a concept without referents, contrasts, or non-examples.
- Preferring stable user types when situated states fit the evidence.
- Calling studies mutually validating without a validation design.
- Writing survey reports as observed behavior.
- Writing movement, attention, or engagement traces as learning outcomes without a learning measure.
- Adding an AI intervention merely to make a design implication sound like HCI.
- Keeping every interesting result in the main paper.
- Reopening the intellectual frame during submission QA without a validity reason.
- Editing many logical units at once when the user needs traceable changes.
- Expanding claims to sound more academic.
- Polishing a sentence whose reasoning remains unresolved.
